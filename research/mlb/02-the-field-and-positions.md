# The field and the positions
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season. Primary S1 (Official Baseball Rules 2026); S2 (2025) for comparison; glossary S4-S44 as fallback. Citation `[S1 R2.01, p.14]` = source, rule, PDF page.

## The diamond (infield) and the outfield
The infield is a 90-foot square. The outfield is the area between two foul lines formed by extending two sides of that square. [S1 R2.01, p.14] Home base is at one corner; first, second and third base are the other corners, 90 feet apart along the base lines. [S1 R2.01, p.14]
Layout method in the rules: from home base measure 127 feet 3 3/8 inches toward second base; the diagonal between first and third is also 127 ft 3 3/8 in. [S1 R2.01, p.14]
Distance to the nearest fence: 250 feet or more from home base on fair territory is required; 320 feet or more along the foul lines and 400 feet or more to center field is "preferable." [S1 R2.01, p.14] For fields built by a professional club after 1 June 1958: at least 325 feet to the foul-line fences and 400 feet to center. [S1 R2.01 NOTE (a), p.15] (Glossary says the same and that some clubs were allowed shorter parks. [S6])
The rules recommend the line from home through the pitcher's plate to second base run East-Northeast. [S1 R2.01, p.14] (Desirable, not required.)
Example: no two major league parks are identical, but these dimensions must be uniform. [S6]

## Fair and foul territory
The infield and outfield, including the boundary lines, are fair territory; all other area is foul territory. [S1 R2.01, p.14] Foul lines are part of fair territory. [S1 Definitions "FAIR TERRITORY", p.164] Foul territory is the part outside the first and third base lines, extended to the fence and upward. [S1 Definitions "FOUL TERRITORY", p.165]
A **fair ball** is a batted ball that, for example, settles on fair ground between home and first or home and third, or bounds past first or third on or over fair territory, or touches first, second or third base, or first falls on fair territory on or beyond first/third, or passes out of the playing field in flight while over fair territory. [S1 Definitions "A FAIR BALL", p.163] When it is on the ground, the ball must be touching fair territory, not just over it. [S1 Definitions "A FAIR BALL", p.163; new in 2026, see conflicts]
A **foul ball** is the opposite case: it settles in foul territory between home and first/third, or bounds past first or third over foul territory, or first lands in foul territory beyond first/third, or touches a player or umpire while over foul territory. [S1 Definitions "A FOUL BALL", p.165]
Glossary plain version: foul lines and foul poles mark fair territory; a ball that first touches the ground beyond first or third base is fair if it is on or inside the lines (the lines and poles count as fair). [S12, S13]
Fair/foul is judged by where the ball is relative to the line (and the pole), not where the fielder stands. [S1 Definitions "A FAIR BALL"/"A FOUL BALL", p.163-165]
All fielders except the catcher must be in fair territory when the ball is put in play. [S1 R5.02, p.30]

## Home plate, bases, pitcher's plate, mound
- **Home plate:** a five-sided slab of whitened rubber; 17-inch square with two corners removed so one edge is 17 in. long, two adjacent sides are 8 1/2 in. and the other two sides are 12 in., meeting at a point; the point is set at the intersection of the lines to first and third base and the 17-inch edge faces the pitcher. [S1 R2.02, p.15]
- **Bases (first, second, third):** white canvas or rubber-covered bags, 18 inches square, 3 to 5 inches thick, soft-filled; first and third bags are entirely inside the infield, second is centered on second base. [S1 R2.03, p.16] Glossary: bases were 15 in. square before 2023. [S7]
- **Pitcher's plate (the "rubber"):** a whitened rubber slab 24 inches by 6 inches, 60 feet 6 inches from the rear point of home plate. [S1 R2.04, p.16] It is 10 inches above the level of home plate; the slope from 6 inches in front of the plate to 6 feet toward home is 1 inch per foot. [S1 R2.01, p.14]
- **Other marked areas:** catcher's box, batters' boxes, coaches' boxes, the three-foot first base lane and the next batter's boxes are laid out in the diagrams of Appendices 1 and 2. [S1 R2.01, p.14; diagrams on S1 p.174-175 (Appendix 1 shows an "18' circle" at the mound)] The glossary also says the mound sits within an 18-foot diameter circle. [S6]
- **Grass lines:** the grass line of the outer boundary of the infield dirt is a 95-foot radius from the center of the pitcher's plate (average between 94 and 96 feet counts as compliant). [S1 R2.01(a), p.15] Other grass and dirt shapes are up to each club. [S1 R2.01, p.15]
- **Base lines and foul lines** are marked with paint or chalk (white). [S1 R2.01, p.14-15]
- **Benches (dugouts):** one for each team, at least 25 feet from the base lines. [S1 R2.05, p.16]
- **Base coaches:** the team at bat has two coaches on the field, one near first base and one near third, staying inside the coach's box (S1 adds penalties for leaving it while the pitcher is on the plate). [S1 R5.03(a)-(c), p.32-33]

## The nine defensive positions
Nine players per side. [S1 R1.01, p.13] The rulebook defines the roles pitcher (the fielder who delivers the pitch to the batter) [S1 Definitions "A PITCHER", p.169], catcher (the fielder who takes his position back of home base) [S1 Definitions "The CATCHER", p.162], infielder (a fielder who occupies a position in the infield) [S1 Definitions "An INFIELDER", p.166] and outfielder (a fielder in the outfield, the area most distant from home base) [S1 Definitions "An OUTFIELDER", p.168]. It does not list the nine by name in one place (UNVERIFIED as a closed list from S1). The glossary position pages give each:
| Position | Where / what (glossary) |
|---|---|
| Pitcher (starting pitcher) | stands on the mound in the center of the infield, 60 ft 6 in. from home plate; starters begin the game on the mound [S41; S1 R2.04, p.16] |
| Catcher | crouches directly behind home plate, receives the pitches, throws to bases to stop steals [S33]; "the battery" = pitcher and catcher [S1 Definitions "The BATTERY", p.159] |
| First baseman | to the right of the first-base bag and toward the back of the infield dirt, or on the bag after a batter reaches first; catches throws to force out runners at first [S34] |
| Second baseman | between first and second base (closer to second), toward the back of the infield dirt [S35] |
| Shortstop | between the third baseman and second base; called captain of the infield [S36] |
| Third baseman | near the third-base bag; fields grounders, line drives, pop flies and bunts near third [S37] |
| Left fielder | left part of the outfield grass as seen from home plate [S38] |
| Center fielder | middle of the outfield; has the most ground to cover; called captain of the outfield [S39] |
| Right fielder | right part of the outfield grass as seen from home plate; often the strongest outfield arm [S40] |
Where fielders may stand: any fielder except pitcher and catcher may stand anywhere in fair territory, with limits (below). [S1 R5.02(c), p.30] The pitcher must take his legal position while delivering [S1 R5.02(b), p.30] and the catcher stands behind the plate (but may leave to catch a pitch or make a play). [S1 R5.02(a), p.30]
**Infield positioning limit (shift limit).** When the pitcher begins his delivery, the defense must have at least four players besides pitcher and catcher with both feet completely in front of the outer boundary of the infield dirt, at least two on each side of second base; the two infielders on each side of second may not switch sides during the inning (except at substitutions). [S1 R5.02(c)(i)-(iii), p.30-31] Glossary: this started in 2023 and prevents four-outfielder alignments. [S9]
**Designated hitter:** bats for the pitcher and does not play defense. [S1 R5.11(a)(1), p.74; S42] 
Example (batting side): the offense's players are "batters" while in the box and "runners" after, [S1 Definitions "A BATTER"/"A RUNNER", p.159, 169; S43, S44].

## Essential terms (mini-glossary)
Rule definitions (S1 "Definitions of Terms", p.159-172) unless otherwise marked.
- **Appeal:** a fielder's claim that the offense violated a rule. [S1 p.159]
- **Balk:** an illegal act by the pitcher with a runner on base, entitling runners to advance one base. [S1 p.159]
- **Ball / Strike / Strike zone:** see note 01. [S1 p.159, p.170]
- **Base on balls (walk):** first base awarded after four balls (or an intentional-walk signal). [S1 p.159]
- **Batter / Batter-runner / Runner:** batter takes his position in the box; batter-runner is the player who just finished his time at bat until he is out or the play ends; a runner is an offensive player advancing toward, touching or returning to a base. [S1 p.159, 169]
- **Bunt:** a batted ball not swung at, intentionally met with the bat and tapped slowly within the infield. [S1 p.160]
- **Dead ball / Live ball:** dead = temporarily out of play by a legal suspension; live = in play. [S1 p.162, 167] ("Time" is the umpire's announcement of a legal interruption. [S1 p.171]) Glossary: dead balls are frequent, e.g. foul balls, hit-by-pitch. [S18]
- **Double play / triple play:** two (three) offensive players put out on continuous action without an error between putouts. [S1 p.162, 171]
- **Error:** see note 01 [S26].
- **Fielder's choice:** a fielder handles a fair grounder and throws to another base to try to put out a preceding runner instead of the batter-runner. [S1 p.164]
- **Fly ball / Ground ball / Line drive:** high in the air / rolls or bounces close to the ground / goes sharp and direct from the bat to a fielder without touching the ground. [S1 p.164, 165, 168]
- **Force play:** see note 01. [S1 p.164; S11]
- **Foul tip:** a batted ball that goes sharp and direct from the bat to the catcher and is legally caught; it is a strike and the ball stays in play. [S1 p.165; S16]
- **Home team:** the team on whose grounds the game is played (neutral site: chosen by agreement). [S1 p.165]
- **Infield fly:** a fair fly ball (not a line drive or bunt) that an infielder can catch with ordinary effort, with first and second, or first, second and third occupied, before two outs; the umpire declares it and the batter is out. [S1 p.166; S1 R5.09(a)(5), p.54; S17]
- **Inning / Out / Run:** see note 01. [S1 p.167, 168, 169]
- **Out:** see note 01. [S1 p.168]
- **Pitch:** a ball delivered to the batter by the pitcher; a throw is any other propelling of the ball by one player to another. [S1 p.169, p.171]
- **Plate appearance vs at-bat:** a plate appearance is each completed turn batting, whatever the result; an official at-bat counts only certain results (walks and sacrifices excluded). [S32, S31]
- **Regulation game:** nine innings (see note 01). [S1 R7.01(a), p.104; S14]
- **Safe:** the umpire's declaration that a runner is entitled to the base he was trying for. [S1 p.169]
- **Tag:** see note 01. [S1 p.170]
- **Wild pitch:** one so high, so low or so wide that it cannot be handled with ordinary effort by the catcher. [S1 p.172]
- **Walk-off, DH, etc.:** DH defined above [S15]; "walk-off" not researched.

## Source conflicts
1. S1 (2026) vs S2 (2025): R2.01-2.05 text is the same in both (S2 p.14-16 = S1 p.14-16, except the appendix reference "page 156" in S2 p.14 becomes "page 162" in S1 p.14, and "Diagram" becomes "Appendix" wording). Diagrams: Appendix 1 is on S1 PDF p.174 (printed p.162) and on S2 PDF p.172 (printed p.160). New in S1: Appendix 6 (runner retouch diagrams). [S1 "Summary of Rule Changes for 2026", p.6]
2. FAIR BALL definition: S1 p.163 adds the sentence about ground contact and "any part of" first/third base in the comment; S2 p.160-161 lacks it. S1 wins (newer).
3. R5.03: S1 p.32-33 adds penalties for coaches leaving the box; S2 p.32-33 had only "return to the box or be removed."
4. Outfield fence "250 feet" (S1 p.14) vs "325/400 for parks built after 1958" (S1 p.15): not a contradiction; the second is a stricter note for parks built after 1958. S6 repeats the 325/400.
5. Glossary "Field Dimensions" says the "plate is placed 18 inches behind the center of the mound" and "surrounded by a level area 5 feet by 34 inches" [S6]; these details were not found in the rule text read (S1 R2.01-2.04). Treat as secondary, UNVERIFIED against rules. The Appendix 1 diagram (S1 p.174) was not re-checked for them.
6. Shift limit: glossary says "four infielders must be within the boundary of the infield" [S9], the rule says four players (besides pitcher and catcher) in front of the outer boundary of the infield dirt, two on each side [S1 R5.02(c), p.30-31]. The rule wins; the glossary is a simplification.
7. S3 (2019): not used here (no relevant section read); pre-dates shift limits and 18-inch bases.
