# Basic rules of baseball
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season. Primary source S1 (Official Baseball Rules 2026). Citation format: `[S1 R5.05(a)(1), p.36]` = source, rule, PDF page. Text of every rule cited here is identical in S2 (2025) unless the "Source conflicts" section says otherwise (pages differ between S1 and S2 after p.24; S2 pages are only given where useful).

## The objective
Two teams of nine players play on an enclosed field, under a manager and one or more umpires. [S1 R1.01, p.13]
The team on offense tries to make its batter a runner and to make its runners advance. [S1 R1.02, p.13] The team on defense tries to stop batters from becoming runners and stop runners from advancing around the bases. [S1 R1.03, p.13]
A batter who becomes a runner and touches all the bases legally scores one run for his team. [S1 R1.04, p.13] The winner is the team that scores more runs than the opponent at the end of a regulation game. [S1 R1.05-1.06, p.13]
Example (simple version): more runs wins; a run = a player goes batter, first, second, third, home, in that order. [S1 Definitions "A RUN (or SCORE)", p.169]

## Innings, outs and who bats when
An inning is the part of a game in which the teams take turns on offense and defense, with three putouts for each team. Each team's turn at bat is a half-inning. [S1 Definitions "An INNING", p.167]
An out is one of the three required retirements of the offensive team during its time at bat. [S1 Definitions "An OUT", p.168] (Glossary says the same in plain words: three outs retire the side. [S29])
The game starts when the umpire calls "Play": the home team takes the field, and the first batter of the visiting team steps into the batter's box. [S1 R5.01(a), p.30] So the visitors bat first in every inning (top half) and the home team second. [S1 R5.01(a), p.30; R7.01(e)(1), p.106 (the visitors "complete [their] half of the ninth inning" before the home team's half)]. The rulebook says "half-inning"; it does not use "top" and "bottom" for these rules (searched), so those words are everyday usage, not sourced.
A run counts only if the runner touches all bases before the third out. Exception: no run scores on a play where the third out is (1) the batter-runner before he touches first, (2) any runner forced out, or (3) a preceding runner out for failing to touch a base. [S1 R5.08(a), p.51]
Example: one out, Jones on second, Smith on first; Brown hits safely, Jones scores, Smith is out at home (two outs), but Brown missed first and is out on appeal (three outs). Because the third out was made by the batter-runner before touching first, Jones's run does not count. [S1 R5.08 Comment "Approved Ruling", p.52]

## The batting order
Each player of the offensive team bats in the order in which his name appears in his team's batting order, and this order continues all game unless a substitute replaces a player (the substitute takes that place). [S1 R5.04(a)(1)-(2), p.33]
Each new inning after the first starts with the player after the last player who legally finished his time at bat in the previous inning. [S1 R5.04(a)(3), p.33] A batter has finished his time at bat when he is put out or becomes a runner. [S1 R5.04(c), p.36]
Before the game each manager gives the umpire-in-chief his batting order; once the umpire hands out the copies, the orders are set and no substitutions are allowed except as the rules provide. [S1 R4.03, p.24-25]
A "designated hitter" may bat in place of the pitcher for the whole game; he must be chosen before the game and listed on the lineup card. [S1 R5.11(a)(1), p.74] The glossary says the DH became universal (both leagues) as part of the 2022-26 CBA; the NL adopted it then. [S15] (Details in a later note.)

## Balls, strikes and the strike zone
The batter stands in the batter's box; the pitcher throws a pitch and the batter may swing or not. [S1 R5.01(c), p.30]
- A **ball** is a pitch that does not enter the strike zone and is not swung at; a pitch that bounces through the zone is also a ball. [S1 Definitions "A BALL", p.159]
- A **strike** is a legal pitch that the umpire calls when it (a) is swung at and missed, (b) is not swung at but any part of the ball passes through any part of the strike zone, (c) is fouled by the batter with fewer than two strikes, (d) is bunted foul, (e) touches the batter as he swings, (f) touches the batter in flight in the strike zone, or (g) is a foul tip. [S1 Definitions "A STRIKE", p.170]
- The **strike zone** is the area over home plate whose upper limit is a horizontal line at the midpoint between the top of the shoulders and the top of the uniform pants, and whose lower limit is a line at the hollow beneath the kneecap. It is judged from the batter's stance as he prepares to swing. [S1 Definitions "The STRIKE ZONE", p.170; diagram Appendix 5] The MLB glossary adds that part of the ball must cross over part of home plate in that area for a strike call. [S5]
- Three strikes: the batter is out (see strikeout below). Four balls: the batter walks (see below).
Example: a pitch is fouled off with 0 or 1 strike, so it is a strike; with two strikes a foul ball is not a strike (it cannot be the third strike). [S1 Definitions "A STRIKE" (c), p.170; S19 "A foul ball counts as a strike, but it cannot be the third and final strike"]

## Ways the batter becomes a runner
He becomes a runner when:
- **He hits a fair ball.** [S1 R5.05(a)(1), p.36] A hit credited to the batter: single, double, triple (reaches 1st/2nd/3rd without an error or an attempt to put out another runner). [S22, S23, S24] A **home run** is a fair ball that passes over the fence or into the stands at a distance of 250 feet or more from home base; the batter scores after touching all bases legally. [S1 R5.05(a)(5), p.37; S25 glossary]
- **A walk (base on balls):** four balls are called. [S1 R5.05(b)(1), p.38] He gets first base without risk of being put out, provided he advances to and touches first. [S1 R5.05(b), p.38] A walk can also be awarded when the defensive manager signals an intentional walk to the umpire. [S1 Definitions "A BASE ON BALLS", p.159]
- **Hit by pitch:** he is touched by a pitched ball he is not trying to hit, unless the ball is in the strike zone when it touches him, or he makes no attempt to avoid it. [S1 R5.05(b)(2), p.38] In the strike zone it is a strike whether or not he tries to avoid it. [S1 R5.05(b)(2), p.38] Glossary plain version: a batter hit by a pitch without swinging is awarded first base; strikes supersede hit-by-pitches. [S21]
- **Dropped third strike:** the third strike is not caught, and first base is empty (or first is occupied with two outs). [S1 R5.05(a)(2), p.36]
- **Interference by the catcher or a fielder** with the batter (the offense may decline the penalty and take the play). [S1 R5.05(b)(3), p.38]
- **Reaching on an error:** a fielder who fails to make an out on a play an average fielder should make is charged with an error (decided by the official scorer). [S26] "Ordinary effort" = the effort of a fielder of average skill at that position. [S1 Definitions "ORDINARY EFFORT", p.168]
Example (walk with bases loaded): the batter-runner must touch first, and the runner forced from third must touch home before the game can be ruled over if the winning run is scored this way. [S1 R5.08(b), p.51-52]

## Ways to be out
A batter is out when (among other cases):
- **Strikeout:** a third strike is legally caught by the catcher ("legally caught" = in the catcher's glove before the ball touches the ground). [S1 R5.09(a)(2) + Comment, p.54] Also out when he bunts foul on the third strike. [S1 R5.09(a)(4), p.54] If the catcher does not catch the third strike, first base is occupied and there are fewer than two outs, the batter is out anyway. [S1 R5.09(a)(3), p.54] If first base is empty (or two outs) he may run to first. [S1 R5.05(a)(2), p.36; S19]
- **Flyout:** his fair or foul fly ball (other than a foul tip) is legally caught by a fielder. [S1 R5.09(a)(1), p.54] Catch = secure possession in hand or glove of a ball in flight and firmly holding it, without using cap, protector, pocket or other uniform part; it is not a catch if he drops it because of a collision with a player or wall or falling. [S1 Definitions "A CATCH", p.160]
- **Out at first (groundout):** after a hit (or third strike), the batter is out if he or first base is tagged before he touches first. [S1 R5.09(a)(10), p.55] Glossary: a groundout is a ball hit on the ground to a fielder who throws to or steps on first. [S28]
A runner is out when:
- **Tag out:** he is tagged while the ball is alive and he is off his base (a batter-runner who overruns first base and returns immediately cannot be tagged out). [S1 R5.09(b)(4), p.58] A **tag** = a fielder touching a runner with the ball (or with the hand or glove holding the ball), holding the ball securely, or touching a base with his body while holding the ball. [S1 Definitions "A TAG", p.170]
- **Force out:** a force play is a play in which a runner legally loses his right to occupy a base because the batter became a runner. [S1 Definitions "A FORCE PLAY", p.164] The runner is out if he or the next base is tagged before he touches that next base. [S1 R5.09(b)(6), p.59] Once a following runner is put out on a force play, the force is removed and the other runner must be tagged. [S1 R5.09(b)(6), p.59] Glossary: first base has the most force plays, because the batter is forced whenever he puts a fair ball in play that is not caught in the air. [S11]
- Other ways exist (running more than three feet from the base path to avoid a tag, abandoning the bases, interference, leaving too soon on a caught fly, etc.). [S1 R5.09(b)(1)-(7), p.57-60] Not covered here.
Example (force vs tag): with a runner on first, one out, a ground ball to the first baseman: he touches first (batter out), the force is removed, so the runner going to second must be tagged. [S1 Definitions "(Force Play) Comment", p.164]

## How a game ends
A regulation game is nine innings, unless extended because of a tie or shortened (the home team needs none or only part of its ninth half-inning, or the umpire or the Commissioner's Office calls the game). [S1 R7.01(a), p.104]
- If the home team is ahead after the visitors finish the top of the ninth, the game ends there. [S1 R7.01(e)(1), p.106]
- If the visitors are ahead after nine completed innings, it ends. [S1 R7.01(e)(2), p.106]
- If the home team scores the winning run in its half of the ninth (or of an extra inning), the game ends immediately when that run scores. Exception: a home run out of the park by the last batter lets the batter and all runners score and the game ends when the batter touches home. [S1 R7.01(e)(3) + Exception, p.106]
- The winning run scored on a walk/hit batter with bases full only counts once the forced runner touches home and the batter-runner touches first. [S1 R5.08(b), p.51-52]
- A called game is a regulation game if five innings have been completed, or the home team has more runs in 4 or 4 1/3 half-innings than the visitors in five completed half-innings, or the home team scores in the fifth to tie. [S1 R7.01(d), p.104-105] Glossary plain version: official after the visitors have made 15 outs with the home team leading, or the home team has made 15 outs. [S14]

## Extra innings
If the score is tied after nine completed innings, play continues until (1) the visitors have more total runs than the home team at the end of a completed inning, or (2) the home team scores the winning run in an uncompleted inning. [S1 R7.01(b)(1), p.104]
Every half-inning after the ninth starts with a runner on second base: the player in the batting order just before that half-inning's leadoff hitter (or a pinch-runner for him; if that player is the pitcher, it may be the player before the pitcher). [S1 R7.01(b)(2)(B), p.104] The leadoff batter stays the one who would lead off anyway. [S1 R7.01(b)(2)(A), p.104]
Example from the rule: if the number five hitter is due to lead off the tenth, the number four hitter (or pinch-runner) starts on second. [S1 R7.01(b)(2)(B), p.104]
For earned-run counting, that runner is treated as having reached second on a fielding error, but no error is charged to anyone. [S1 R7.01(b)(2)(C), p.105] Glossary: the "automatic runner" began as a 2020 pandemic protocol, stayed in 2021-22, and was made permanent by the Joint Competition Committee before the 2023 season; it says the rule applies only to regular-season games. [S10] (The rulebook text itself has no regular-season-only wording in the section read; see conflicts.)

## Recent rule changes the sources mention (with years)
- 2023: bigger bases (18 in square, from 15 in), pitch timer, defensive shift limits. [S7, S8, S9] (Rule text for bases: 18 in square, S1 R2.03, p.16.)
- 2024: pitch timer with runners on reduced to 18 seconds. [S8]
- 2025: shift-restriction penalty tweak (batter gets first base if the illegally positioned infielder first touches the ball; otherwise a ball). [S9; S2 "Summary of Rule Changes for 2025", p.5; S1 R5.02(c) Penalty, p.31-32]
- 2026 edition changes in the rulebook (lineup-card errors, base coaches, runner retouch, catch/fair ball/tag definitions, pitching-change signalling, three-batter minimum, spectator interference, catcher set-up, Appendix 6): [S1 "Summary of Rule Changes for 2026", p.5-6]. The ones relevant to basics are listed in "Source conflicts".
- 2023 universal DH and automatic runner: see [S15], [S10].

## Source conflicts
1. **S1 (2026) vs S2 (2025), sections used here.** Text of R1, R2.01-2.04, R5.01, R5.04(a), R5.05, R5.08, R5.09(a)(2)-(4),(10), R5.09(b)(1),(4),(6), R7.01 and definitions BALL, STRIKE, STRIKE ZONE, FORCE PLAY, INNING, OUT, RUN is word-for-word the same (compared by script). Differences found:
   - **R4.03 lineup cards (S1 p.24-26 vs S2 p.24-26):** S2 said players "as a courtesy" list positions and potential substitutes and that failing to list a substitute does not make him ineligible. S1 says each card should list positions, and "a player or pitcher not listed on the lineup card is not permitted to enter that game," with procedures for an improper player. S1 wins (newer).
   - **R5.09(a)(1) Comment and CATCH Comment (S2 p.54, p.159) vs S1 p.160 and Definitions:** the dugout/boundary catch language moved into the Definitions; S1 adds a "Catches Proximate To A Boundary" part (a fielder may not step or jump into the stands/dugout to catch a ball) and says a catch does not require the ball in the throwing hand on transfer. S1 wins.
   - **CATCH definition last sentence:** S2 p.159 "If the fielder has made the catch and drops the ball while in the act of making a throw following the catch, the ball shall be adjudged to have been caught." S1 p.160 rewrites it around "complete control" and intentionally opening the glove to transfer to the throwing hand. S1 wins.
   - **FAIR BALL (S2 p.160-161 vs S1 p.163):** S1 adds "When in contact with the ground, a ball must be in contact with fair territory and not merely over fair territory in order to be adjudged to be fair," and "any part of" first or third base in the comment.
   - **TAG (S2 p.168 vs S1 p.170):** S1 adds "If the fielder has the ball in his bare hand pressed against his glove while the tag is applied, it should be viewed as a legal tag," and a Tag Comment about malicious contact by a runner.
   - **R5.06(b)(1) (S2 p.40 vs S1 p.40):** S1 adds that a runner has "passed" a base when both feet are beyond it, and a retouch requirement (see Appendix 6). Not used in basics.
   - **R5.03 (base coaches):** S1 p.32-33 adds penalties (warning, then removal) for a coach who leaves the box while the pitcher is in contact with the plate.
   - Page numbers: S1 and S2 are page-aligned up to p.24; later S1 pages are shifted by 0-2 pages (e.g. R5.08(a) is p.51 in both; R5.09(a)(1) starts p.54 in S1 vs p.53 in S2; R7.01 p.104 in S1 vs p.103 in S2; STRIKE ZONE p.170 in S1 vs p.167-168 in S2).
2. **Pitch timer vs R5.07(c) (S8 vs S1 p.50).** The rulebook (S1 and S2) says that with the bases unoccupied the pitcher must deliver within **12 seconds** after receiving the ball, else the umpire calls "Ball." [S1 R5.07(c), p.50] The glossary says the timer is 15 seconds (bases empty) and 18 seconds (runners on, since 2024). [S8] Neither source explains the discrepancy. The rulebook contains no text about a "pitch timer" or "pitch clock" (searched). Prefer S1 for what it says, but treat the 12 s rule as possibly superseded in practice. UNVERIFIED which one is in force. Need MLB's separate regulations (not in the sources).
3. **Automatic runner scope (S10 vs S1 p.104).** Glossary: regular season only. Rulebook R7.01(b)(2) (S1 p.104-105): no such limit in the text. Not resolved. UNVERIFIED for postseason.
4. **S3 (2019 manual) vs S1.** The 2019 manual (Part II, rule interpretations, cites rule numbers such as 5.09(b)(12) and 5.09(c)(2) that also appear in S2) was only used for cross-checking the force-play example (S3 PDF p.60, printed p.22: "Run counts. This is a time play, NOT a force play"; same ruling as S1 Definitions "(Force Play) Comment", p.164-165). The 2019 manual predates the 2020+ changes (automatic runner, shift limits, pitch timer, etc.), so S1 wins whenever they could differ.
5. **Bases.** Glossary says bases were 15 in square and became 18 in. in 2023 [S7]; rulebook says 18 in. now [S1 R2.03, p.16]. Consistent.
6. **Outfield fence minimum.** S1 R2.01 (p.14): "250 feet or more" with 320/400 "preferable"; the NOTE says fields built after 1 June 1958 need at least 325 ft (foul lines) and 400 ft (center). Glossary repeats the 325/400 rule and notes some clubs were allowed shorter. [S6] Consistent, but the 250 ft figure is also the home-run threshold in R5.05(a)(5).
